## Sales Pipeline Analysis: where B2B deals are won and lost

An end-to-end analysis of a B2B sales pipeline with **2,000 leads** across five lead sources, six industries and five sales reps. Using **SQL** and **Python**, I trace each lead from first contact to a won or lost deal, find where the funnel leaks, and turn the numbers into practical recommendations for a sales team.

> The dataset is **fully synthetic** and generated by `src/generate_data.py` with a fixed random seed, so every number in this README can be reproduced exactly. No real company or customer data is used.

---

## Business questions

1. Where in the funnel do we lose the most leads?
2. Which lead sources bring the best customers, not just the most leads?
3. Which company sizes and industries should we focus on?
4. How big are the performance differences between sales reps?

## Key findings

### 1. The biggest leak is between first contact and a meeting

![Sales funnel](charts/1_funnel.png)

| Stage | Leads | Conversion from previous stage |
|---|---|---|
| Lead | 2,000 | |
| Contacted | 1,370 | 68.5% |
| Meeting | 380 | **27.7%** |
| Proposal | 202 | 53.2% |
| Won | 77 | 38.1% |

Only about **1 in 4 contacted leads agrees to a meeting.** Once a meeting happens, more than half get a proposal and almost 4 in 10 proposals are won. **Improving the first conversation has the biggest effect on revenue.**

### 2. Referrals and inbound leads convert up to 4× better than cold outreach

![Win rate by source](charts/2_win_rate_by_source.png)

| Source | Leads | Win rate | Revenue won | Avg. days to close |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Referral | 198 | **8.1%** | €383k | **41** |
| Inbound Website | 283 | 5.7% | €395k | **37** |
| LinkedIn Outreach | 528 | 4.2% | €408k | 58 |
| Cold Email | 613 | 2.4% | €522k | 58 |
| Cold Call | 378 | 2.1% | €135k | 54 |

- **Referrals win 8.1% of the time**, almost 4× more often than cold calls (2.1%), and close about **2–3 weeks faster.**
- **Cold email brings the most revenue (€522k) only because of volume.** It has the most leads but one of the lowest win rates.
- **LinkedIn outreach (4.2%) clearly beats cold email (2.4%)** as an outbound channel.

### 3. Large companies win more often and pay far more

| Company size | Win rate | Avg. deal size |
|---|---|---|
| 1000+ employees | **5.0%** | **€61.7k** |
| 201–1000 | 3.7% | €35.0k |
| 51–200 | 4.6% | €14.2k |
| 1–50 | 2.9% | €6.6k |

Companies with **1,000+ employees have the highest win rate and deals almost 10× larger** than small companies. Small companies (1–50) take the most effort for the least return.

### 4. SaaS and e-commerce are the strongest industries

| Industry | Win rate | Revenue won |
|---|---|---|
| SaaS | **6.5%** | €563k |
| E-commerce | 4.8% | €535k |
| Healthcare | 3.7% | €243k |
| Logistics | 3.5% | €234k |
| Fintech | 3.0% | €128k |
| Manufacturing | **1.7%** | €140k |

### 5. Rep performance differs by more than 2×

![Revenue by rep](charts/3_revenue_by_rep.png)

The best reps win **5.1%** of their leads, the weakest **2.1%**, with similar lead volumes (375–420 each). The gap is a coaching opportunity: sharing what the top reps do in first calls could lift the whole team.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Fix the first conversation.** Contact-to-meeting is the weakest step (27.7%). Test better call openers and email subject lines, and track meeting rate weekly per rep.
2. **Build a referral programme.** Referrals win 4× more often and close faster. Ask every won customer for one introduction.
3. **Shift outbound effort from cold email to LinkedIn.** LinkedIn outreach converts about 75% better (4.2% vs 2.4%).
4. **Prioritise companies with 200+ employees,** especially 1000+, and qualify out very small companies earlier.
5. **Pair weaker reps with top performers** for call shadowing and shared scripts.


## Project structure

```
sales-pipeline-analysis/
├── data/leads.csv           # synthetic dataset (2,000 leads)
├── sql/analysis.sql         # all SQL queries, commented
├── src/generate_data.py     # builds the dataset (fixed seed = 42)
├── src/analyse.py           # runs SQL in SQLite, saves tables and charts
├── results/                 # query results as CSV
└── charts/                  # PNG charts used in this README
```

## Data dictionary

| Column | Description |
|---|---|
| `lead_id` | Unique lead ID |
| `created_date` | Date the lead entered the pipeline (2025) |
| `source` | Referral, Inbound Website, LinkedIn Outreach, Cold Email, Cold Call |
| `industry` | SaaS, E-commerce, Fintech, Logistics, Healthcare, Manufacturing |
| `company_size` | Number of employees |
| `country` | Germany, Netherlands, France, UK, Ireland, Austria |
| `sales_rep` | Assigned rep (anonymised) |
| `stage` | Current status: Lead, Contacted, Meeting, Won or Lost |
| `furthest_stage` | Furthest step reached before the outcome |
| `deal_value_eur` | Proposal value in euros (0 if no proposal) |
| `days_to_close` | Days from lead to won deal (won deals only) |
